# BetterEngineer Copy Brief

Use this file as the source of truth before writing website copy.

## Non-Negotiable Writing Rules

- Never use the em dash character (`—`) in website copy.
- Do not use generic AI-sounding marketing language.
- Prefer direct, concrete language over abstract claims.
- Tie major claims to a mechanism, proof point, or clear outcome.

## Positioning Statement

BetterEngineer builds relationships, not just teams. We connect U.S. companies with pre-vetted senior software engineers from Latin America who embed as trusted long-term collaborators.

## Core Narrative

- Great products come from teams that trust each other and stay together.
- Hiring breaks when speed and volume replace fit, communication, and ownership.
- BetterEngineer is people-first and quality-first, not resume-volume-first.
- Engineers are introduced as integral teammates, not interchangeable contractors.

## What We Solve

- U.S. teams struggle to scale engineering without losing speed or quality.
- Leaders need senior talent that can integrate quickly into real product workflows.
- Traditional outsourcing often creates transactional behavior, weak ownership, and turnover.

## Value Propositions to Reuse

- Senior-only LATAM engineers who work in U.S. time zones.
- Fast initial matching while preserving quality and cultural fit.
- Higher retention through relationship-led matching and onboarding.
- Multi-layer vetting for technical depth, communication, and trust.

## Approved Proof Points

Use these when relevant and accurate for the page context:

- 25,000+ vetted engineers across Latin America.
- 72 hours average time to present initial candidates.
- 38 days average time to hire.
- 3 out of 4 presented candidates are interviewed.
- 21.3 months average engineer tenure.
- 44% average first-year hiring cost savings. This is fully loaded cost versus a U.S. hire (salary, benefits, recruiting, HR). Say what it is compared against only where it fits naturally, for example "44% lower fully loaded cost than a U.S. hire".
- 98% of placements lead to long-term engagements.
- The intro call is 30 minutes (matches Calendly).

### Retired claims (do not use)

## ICP Segments

Always choose the primary ICP before drafting a section.

**Website primary ICP: established companies (ICP 2), read by a VP Engineering or CTO.** Current clients are mostly startups and founders found through the CEO's event networking. The website targets established companies, so default to ICP 2 unless a page is built for another segment.

### ICP 1: Startups (5 to 50 employees)

- **Decision-makers:** Founder, CTO, VP Engineering, engineering manager.
- **Primary goals:** Ship MVP/features quickly, keep team quality high, preserve culture.
- **Primary pains:** Slow hiring, weak remote fit, fear of low-commitment outsourcing.
- **Message angle:** Engineers who care like owners and match startup pace.
- **Proof they want:** Time-zone overlap, startup experience, trust signals, references.

### ICP 2: Established companies (50 to 500 employees)

- **Decision-makers:** VP Engineering, HR/recruiting, CFO, cross-functional leaders.
- **Primary goals:** Predictable delivery, compliance, low onboarding risk, cost control.
- **Primary pains:** Turnover, process friction, legal/compliance complexity.
- **Message angle:** Reliable, process-aligned engineers with low operational drama.
- **Proof they want:** Compliance support, onboarding clarity, retention and ROI metrics.

### ICP 3: Software agencies and consultancies

- **Decision-makers:** Managing partner, VP Engineering, tech lead, account/project manager.
- **Primary goals:** Flexible bandwidth, client satisfaction, utilization, renewals.
- **Primary pains:** Churn, missed deadlines, weak communication with end clients.
- **Message angle:** Client-ready engineers who protect delivery quality and reputation.
- **Proof they want:** Fast ramp, agency-style collaboration, client-facing communication strength.

## Objections and Response Pattern

- "Nearshore means lower quality" -> show vetting layers, seniority standards, and interview conversion.
- "Will they align with our team?" -> explain relationship-led matching and U.S. overlap.
- "Will this add HR or compliance risk?" -> emphasize process, support, and predictable operations.
- "How is this different from staffing firms?" -> stress curated network, quality bar, and long-term retention.

## Tone and Style

- Sound like an experienced operator.
- Favor practical language and concrete nouns.
- Keep sentence flow natural and readable.
- Use confident language without hype.
- Structure sections as: problem, approach, proof, next step.

## Phrases to Avoid

- revolutionize
- unlock potential
- seamless
- game-changing
- cutting-edge
- broad claims with no evidence
